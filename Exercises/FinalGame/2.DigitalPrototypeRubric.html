About this milestone

RememberA prototype tests an idea, demonstrates or perfects a behavior, or builds a well-structured class. Each one should be an isolated, simple program with a precise purpose. Unless there's a specific reason, one prototype should not test several ideas at once.
Expect to see
  • Each prototype's purpose: the problem it tests
  • The prototype running
  • What the team learned, and how it will be used in the game
Not expected yet
  • The whole game
  • Final art or audio
  • Menus, levels or polish

How to score

  • Enter one whole number for each category, from 5 (best) to 1.
  • When a category has more than one criterion, weigh them together. If you're unsure, average them and round to the nearest whole number.
  • The descriptions are anchors, not checklists: pick the column that fits best.
  • Score what you saw today, not what the team says it will add later.
  • Most criteria are subjective. Apply your own judgment, but apply it the same way to every team.

Hints on preparation

This is your “proof of concept”! You demo, we watch. You need to prove your game is doable: find and solve your own problems.
